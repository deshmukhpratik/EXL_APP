A Where Do the Curves Come From?

The engine needs a monthly Default Rate (DCOR) and Prepayment Rate (DPOR) for each loan segment. There are three ways to provide them:

📊 Auto-Derived from Tape
The engine extracts actual default and prepayment rates from your performance tape, then fits parametric curves to project beyond observed months.
Default — No Action Needed
📋 Upload Custom Curves
Upload a CSV with your own DCOR/DPOR values by Product × Term × Vintage × MOB. Use this when you have proprietary risk models or actuarial projections.
Manual Upload
⚙ Scenario Adjustments
Apply stress or optimistic multipliers to the base curves to model different economic environments (recession, benign credit cycle, etc.).
Advanced
B How Auto-Derivation Works (Step by Step)

When using the performance tape, the engine builds curves in three steps:

1
Empirical Extraction
Average observed DCOR & DPOR per
Product × Term × Vintage × MOB
from loan-level performance tape.

Covers MOBs 0–24 (observed window)
➔
2
Parametric Tail Fit
DCOR: Exponential decay
a × e-b×t + c
(captures the “hump then decline” default pattern)

DPOR: Constant average
(observed ~1%/month, very flat)
➔
3
Young Vintage Fallback
Vintages with <6 observed months
inherit the curve shape
from the nearest older vintage
in the same Product × Term segment.

Prevents noisy short-history curves

Result: complete DCOR/DPOR curve from MOB 0 to loan maturity (e.g. 72 months) for every segment

C Scenario Multipliers

Scenarios adjust the base curves to model different credit environments:

Scenario Default Rate (DCOR) Prepay Rate (DPOR) Recovery Adjustment Use Case
Base 1.0× (unchanged) 1.0× (unchanged) No change Current economic environment
Stress 1.5× (50% higher defaults) 0.7× (30% fewer prepays) −5pp recovery Recession / downturn modelling
Optimistic 0.7× (30% lower defaults) 1.2× (20% more prepays) +3pp recovery Benign credit cycle / expansion

Part 2 — Monthly Cashflow Waterfall (One Loan, One Month)
What Happens to the Balance Each Month?
Each loan's balance is projected forward from origination to maturity, month by month
Opening Book Balance
The outstanding loan balance at the start of this month
BBB — Beginning Book Balance
▼
Outflows — Money Leaving the Book
Inflows — Money Back to Lender
ED
Defaults
Balance × Monthly Default Rate
← from DCOR curve (varies by month-on-books) → feeds Write-offs, Recoveries, Collection Fees
EWF
Net Write-offs
Defaults − Recoveries
← the portion of defaults that is permanently lost
EUPP
Prepayments
(Balance − Defaults) × Monthly Prepay Rate
← from DPOR curve — borrowers paying off early
ESF + ECF + ERF
Fees & Costs
Servicing: Surviving Bal × 0.06%/mo
Collection: Defaults × 10%
Recovery: Recoveries × 15%
← product parameters (editable per product)
ADJ_EIP
Interest Income
Surviving Balance × Monthly Interest Rate
← from loan APR (e.g. 12% ÷ 12 = 1%/mo)
ESPP
Scheduled Principal
Standard amortisation payment on surviving balance
← from loan term & APR (level-pay amortisation)
ER
Recoveries
Defaults × Recovery Rate
← product parameter (PL: 20%, AL: 40%)
EUPP
Prepayments (returned principal)
Prepaid principal flows back but earns no future interest
← reduces balance, counted as cash returned
Surviving Balance = Opening − Defaults − Prepayments
The performing balance that continues to generate interest & principal
▼
Net Cashflow to Lender
Interest + Principal + Prepayments + Recoveries
− Servicing Fees − Collection Fees − Recovery Fees
NCF — this is the cashflow used in the IRR calculation
Closing Book Balance
Surviving Balance − Scheduled Principal
This becomes next month's Opening Balance
EBB — Ending Book Balance
↻ Closing Balance feeds into next month's Opening Balance
Repeated for every month from origination to loan maturity (e.g. 72 months)
Outflows (losses, prepayments, fees)
Inflows (interest, principal, recoveries)
Net Cashflow (used for IRR)
Balance (carried forward)
After all months are projected, the engine computes
Loan-Level IRR
Each loan's own rate of return based on its cashflow stream
Portfolio IRR
Aggregate return across all loans, weighted by size & timing
Segment IRR
Breakdowns by FICO, vintage, channel, risk grade, etc.
